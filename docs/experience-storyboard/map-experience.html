Shared behavior

Map selection contract

  • Destination labels and the Places list offer equivalent access.
  • Selection starts a bounded walk and opens the destination without requiring the visitor to wait.
  • Closing returns to the preserved map position and invoking control.
  • Direct links, keyboard and touch reach the same content.

The design must preserve existing project links, contact actions and useful legacy URLs.

02 · Interaction storyboard

Every action has a return path.

Choose a journey to step through the proposed states. These are design states, not a demonstration of finished animation.

Interruptions

Selecting another place changes the destination without leaving a stranded animation. Close, Back, Escape and direct navigation always remain available. Focus stays on the control, not on the character.

Failure and recovery

Map or character assets can fail without hiding Places or content. Forms preserve entered text on failure and expose retry. Missing media shows a meaningful text alternative.

Map & landmarks

  • Illustrated paths, distinct recognizable landmarks and explicit labels.
  • Selected destination gets a marker and label, not color alone.
  • Safe character paths never cross content controls.

Panels & navigation

  • Places menu, destination panel, nested story, Back and Close.
  • Visible focus, meaningful headings and keyboard-operable controls.
  • Desktop side panel; mobile bottom sheet or full screen.

Content components

  • Interest disclosure, milestone detail, project card and story.
  • Dated Now entry and direct contact action.
  • Loading, empty, error and recovery states.

Buttons

Each has default, hover, focus, pressed and disabled states; labels describe their action.

Type & spacing

Readable body text and clear headings take priority over pixel styling. Use a consistent spacing scale and test actual fonts and contrast before approval.

Light & dark

Same paths, destinations and content in each theme. Final dark artwork and panel tokens remain to be designed and contrast tested.

04 · Character choreography

Motion has a reason and an ending.

Idle

While reading

Omkar and Stella settle; no constant bounce or distracting loop.

Travel

Place selected

Move on a defined safe path. Stella follows with consistent spacing. Destination content is available immediately.

Arrival

Reach a place

Stop at its anchor and give one brief contextual gesture, then return to rest.

React

Visitor acts

A short pose supports a meaningful interaction, such as a workshop result.

Change direction

New destination

Cancel the old route from the current position; avoid snapping through landmarks or covering controls.

Fallback

Reduced motion / failure

Use a stationary pose or immediately settle. Navigation and all content continue independently.

Motion proof: prepare actual layered artwork and a consistent character rig or sprite set, then test enter → walk → arrive → react → idle on desktop and mobile. Measure frame rate and loading; 60 fps is a target, not a guarantee. Tool selection follows the proof.

Detailed proposed transition rules: character and motion specification.

Open Workshop

Illustrative layout; crop and controls require visual design.

Mobile behavior

  • Show a readable map area and an always-available Places control, rather than shrinking the whole world.
  • Tap a destination; a bottom sheet or full-screen panel presents readable content and close/back actions.
  • Map gestures never trap ordinary scrolling; controls remain reachable without precise dragging.
  • Keyboard and screen-reader visitors can navigate a normal list of destinations.
  • Reduced motion, hidden tab and failed assets retain complete access to content.
06 · Asset & technology boundary

From picture to working world.

Artwork to prepare

  • Background, terrain, paths and landmarks as independently positioned layers.
  • Destination hit areas, anchors and a separate readable interface.
  • Consistent Omkar and Stella character sources and motion poses.
  • Desktop/mobile crops, dark treatment and static fallback.

Candidate implementation

  • Keep Next.js for routes and content, and reuse existing accessible UI primitives.
  • Evaluate Rive for character authoring and Motion for interface choreography; do not commit to either before the proof.
  • Keep normal DOM content and direct links available independently of the map renderer.
  • Measure real bundle, asset weight, frame timing and interaction responsiveness.
Acceptance gate before rebuilding
  • Every destination and nested state is approved, including Back, Close, direct access and failure recovery.
  • Desktop and mobile layouts and light/dark treatments are visually reviewed.
  • Real character artwork and a motion proof are reviewed with interrupted, hidden and reduced-motion states.
  • Keyboard, touch, focus, screen reader and responsive behavior are checked.
  • Measured performance and content accuracy are recorded; 60 fps is a goal with a usable fallback.

Open design work: the reference is not layered artwork; dark theme, mobile composition, detailed key poses and final personal copy are not yet visually approved. This package is a review draft, not permission to rebuild or proof of animation.
